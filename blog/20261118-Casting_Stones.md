# Casting Stones



Following the revelations around Jensen Huang repeatedly having sex with both a direct report and a potential Chinese spy (might as well be plain about it) and subsequently resigning, just SO MANY pixels have been spent defending his actions. I’ve always found defending billionaires to be an odd impulse—they’re **billionaires**, they certainly don’t need your help, and they absolutely couldn’t give less of a shit about you or anyone else. 



> No matter how much you love the billionaires, they’ll never love you back.



That said, one particular strain of that defense is particularly irksome to me. “Why do we hold executives to a higher standard?” plead wannabe thought leaders on LinkedIn. “They’re only human.” Some even lean on Bible quotes to defend his adultery, which is rich: “He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.” (John 8:7) Well, I’ve never screwed around on my wife, and I’m certain I’ve never sold controlled microchips to a foreign adversary (allegedly), and I have this *just perfect* casting stone to hand, so… let’s do this.



You’re **damn right** we hold the officers of multi-trillion dollar corporations to a higher standard. Anything less is suicidally stupid. No one with that level of power and influence is allowed to be only human any more. Through NVIDIA, Jensen Huang controlled 90% of the worldwide AI chip market and a market capitalization that would make it the third largest economy on in the world if it were a country. Jensen is one of the handful of men holding the keys to the future, and he lacks the judgement not to throw that weight into propping up his side piece’s business by selling it chips illegally (allegedly) and fighting legislation aimed at preventing exactly that.



I hope his presumably soon-to-be ex-wife actually does take half of everything he has. She could join MacKenzie Scott in embarrassing her ex by using all that wealth—that she was key in enabling—to do some actual good.
